# Teaching and source rules

Start from Python, Flask, APIs and cloud familiarity. Introduce mathematical prerequisites before neural networks and attention. Teach raw model/tool behavior before LangChain and LangGraph.

Every lesson keeps its prior-knowledge bridge, explanation, real-world analogy, measurable goals, selected source sections, concrete lab steps, acceptance checks, checkpoint explanation and reflection. Unknown terms link to glossary entries and relevant earlier lessons.

Separate required core work from optional reading and deeper experiments. Do not assign entire multi-hour courses as short reading tasks.

Never conflate model weights, conversation context, checkpoint state, long-term memory and caches. Distinguish knowledge graphs, HNSW graphs, computational graphs and workflow graphs.

Do not promise hallucination-free or universally deterministic LLMs. Validate permissions and exact business rules in application code. Mark vendor-reported case studies, simulation outputs and unexecuted labs honestly.

Keep source IDs and direct URLs. Record publisher, available date and checked date. Verify current API and package examples before implementation. Do not invent links, video durations or proprietary architecture claims.
